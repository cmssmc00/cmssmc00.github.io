Research Projects

Language agents · Mar 2026 – Sep 2026

DuoGate: Reliable Language Agents

Advisor: Prof. Pengtao Xie, UC San Diego

Developed a framework that pairs action verification with selective context adaptation for LLM agents, and designed a selective learning pipeline that determines when to learn from execution feedback and which guidance to retain.

On AppWorld with MiniMax M3, raised task completion from 79.8% to 86.3% and reduced recorded token usage by 65% relative to ACE.

Post-training data & evaluation · Sep 2026 – Present

Workplace Task Generation for Language-Agent Post-Training

Advisor: Prof. Pengtao Xie, UC San Diego

Reproduced and extended WorkGenesis to generate executable workplace tasks from O*NET occupations.

On Terminal-Bench-Science, created a pipeline that traces each failure to the agent, task, verifier, or environment; it agreed with all 16 human-audit labels on a held-out set.

Hardened 20 saturated tasks, widening the teacher–student pass-rate gap from about zero to 32 points, then turned the method into a family-based generator that released 380 tasks.

Machine learning for health · Jun 2026 – Present

Pediatric Brain Tumor Classification

Advisor: Prof. Hairong Wang, UT Austin

Trained a hierarchical classifier for ten tumor categories from H&E slides via attention-based multiple-instance learning (MIL).

Added clinical context and immunohistochemistry (IHC) results as likelihood-ratio evidence, applied only when image predictions were uncertain.

In nested five-fold cross-validation on 255 patients, raised ten-class accuracy from 56.5% to 63.5%.

Health data science · Aug 2026 – Nov 2026

Geographic Variation in COPD Care

Advisor: Prof. Hairong Wang, UT Austin

Tested whether neighborhood poverty is associated with COPD hospitalization relative to outpatient care across 44 Austin ZIP codes, using Texas public-use discharge data.

Fit negative-binomial models with an outpatient-encounter offset, adjusted for smoking prevalence from CDC PLACES, and checked for residual spatial autocorrelation.

Each 10-percentage-point increase in poverty was associated with 43% more admissions relative to outpatient care (IRR 1.43).
